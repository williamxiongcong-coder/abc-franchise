Three strengths

What's working.

  • ●Market demand is structural. Specialty cafe spend in Sydney is up 12% YoY; you're riding a tailwind not a fad.
  • ●Unit economics demonstrated. Three profitable venues with documented owner P&L clears the franchise-margin test.
  • ●Code-aware founder. Familiarity with disclosure and cooling-off obligations gets the legal track to draft faster.

Three risks

What needs work.

  • ●No field-support hire. The founder is the field-support team today; that ceiling is reached around venue 4.
  • ●Recruitment engine is referral-only. Will dry up around year two; needs paid acquisition or partnership channels.
  • ●SOPs are partial. Front-of-house documented; back-of-house and supply chain still founder-dependent.

Your three next steps

Where we'd start if we worked together.

  1. 1

    Commission a structured feasibility study to validate unit-economics math at franchise margin (royalty + marketing fund + manager salary).

  2. 2

    Build the SOPs and training architecture that lets someone other than you run a unit end-to-end.

  3. 3

    Identify and bring on the field-support hire before signing your first franchisee — not after.

90-day priority list

What to do in the next quarter.

Days 1–30

Audit existing SOPs. Hire a documentation lead. Commission feasibility brief from a franchise consultant.

Days 31–60

Complete operations manual draft. Identify second-in-command candidate. Validate unit-economics with two pilot territories.

Days 61–90

Engage franchise lawyer for Disclosure draft. Map recruitment funnel. Decide go/no-go on franchise launch for following quarter.
