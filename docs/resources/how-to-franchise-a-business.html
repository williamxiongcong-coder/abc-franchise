How to Franchise a Business.

A practical, four-stage guide to turning a proven business into a franchise network in Australia — from feasibility to first franchisee.

Franchising is not the same as opening more locations. It is the discipline of turning your business into a system that another operator can run — under your brand, to your standard, and at the level of compliance the Australian Franchising Code of Conduct requires.

Done well, it lets you grow a network without funding every venue yourself. Done badly, it bleeds twice — once at the unit, once across the brand. At ABC Franchise we walk founders through four stages.

Stage I — Feasibility & Strategy

Before any documentation, before any recruitment, the question is whether the business should be franchised. We measure readiness across nine factors: unit economics, brand defensibility, operational maturity, documentation, governance, capital, talent, regulatory exposure, growth appetite. Not every "yes" comes immediately — sometimes the answer is "not yet, fix this first."

Outputs: feasibility report, five-year financial model, decision pack.
Timeline: 6–8 weeks.

Stage II — System Architecture

If feasibility checks out, the next step is documenting the system: operations manual, training apparatus, supply protocols, technology stack. This is the substrate that turns your brand from "something only you can operate" into "something a franchisee can operate."

Outputs: operations manual, brand standards, training programme.
Timeline: 10–14 weeks.

Stage III — Legal & Compliance

With the system documented, the legal framework follows. In Australia that means a Disclosure Document (provided 14 days before signing), a Franchise Agreement (the operating contract), and an Information Statement (the Code-mandated plain-English summary). All aligned with the Franchising Code of Conduct and any state-specific obligations (notably Victoria's separate registration regime).

Outputs: full Code-compliant documentation set.
Timeline: 4–6 weeks.

Stage IV — Recruitment & Network Growth

Only now do you recruit. Ideal franchisee profile, discovery day, qualification scorecard, 90-day onboarding programme. The pace at this stage is the founder's pace, not the market's.

Outputs: first 1–3 franchisees signed and trained.
Timeline: 8–12 weeks pilot intake.

What the whole arc looks like

From feasibility kickoff to first franchisee signed, expect six to nine months. Some brands move faster (where documentation is already mature); some go slower (where feasibility surfaces gaps that need fixing first).
